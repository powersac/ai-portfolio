How I run AI work

1. Start with the business goal.What needs to move (revenue, cost, speed or risk) and who owns that number.
2. Define the problem and the finish line.The specific problem, who lives with it, where the number stands today, and the measure that says it's solved, agreed before any tool is chosen.
3. Build the smallest version that proves it.I do the hands-on build myself. I decide up front which calls stay with a person (what gets sent, what gets approved, what counts as an exception) and build that approval into the tool.
4. Measure it, then make it routine.Check it against the measure from step 2 and what it costs to run. If it holds, a named owner on the team runs it and it becomes part of how they operate, not a side project. If it doesn't, stop it.

A few habits run through every example below. Each system shows its work: the job search logs every rejected posting with a reason, and the research drafter flags every sentence that goes beyond what the researcher said. A person stays in the loop, and nothing goes out without my yes. Failures become tests, so a fixed bug cannot come back quietly. Parts get reused, not rebuilt: one set of tools runs the startup's sales, fundraising and weekly CEO review. Security follows the same care: client work runs inside the client's own environment and accounts, and I secure the side projects myself.

Business work comes first, then side projects. Everything is folded so you can scan the list, then open whatever is relevant. Each one opens with the business problem, then how AI is used, then the result. Click any image to see it full screen.

▸Diabetes Research LabA researcher talks, a local model drafts and flags anything not saidLocal AI tool
Draft Zero · Built with Claude

Problem

Finished, figure-ready lab results sit unpublished because nobody has time to write the first draft.

How AI is used

A researcher talks through their figures for about ten minutes. A model running on the lab's own Mac (Qwen) drafts only from what was said and the papers supplied, so no data goes to an outside AI service. It then checks its own draft sentence by sentence and flags every claim the researcher did not make.

Result

A Word draft in the lab's template plus a gap report. It refuses to invent: there is no Discussion section until the researcher records one.

▸Sales Operating SystemA B2B sales rep's AI setup, with a call list agentAgent and web app
sales-os-kit · Built with Claude

Problem

A B2B sales rep loses call days to dead numbers and phone menus, and follow-up slips between calls.

How AI is used

It plugs into the rep's own AI, which walks him through the kit one idea at a time: add, skip, later or research. Green leads go to a phone path, everyone else to a touch plan, and every touch is logged in Pipedrive. A call list agent dials company main lines, works phone menus and dial-by-name directories, and saves the keys to press, so the rep only dials numbers he can reach. It never speaks to a prospect or auto-dials a cell phone. AI drafts and the rep sends.

Result

A kit of skills, touch plans and guardrails, the call list agent's design, and a working demo on simulated phone lines. The client, a B2B sales rep: “This is speeding up my phone call process by 50%.”

▸Recovery SupportA recovery plan you mostly talk toVoice app
First 90 · Built with Claude

Problem

The first 90 days after outpatient treatment are when people most need structure, and when the structure drops away. Each person leaves with a plan, but it lives on paper, and tracking it takes effort most people won't keep up.

How AI is used

The person records a two- to three-minute voice check-in. The AI does the rest. It marks off which parts of the plan got done, scores the day for honesty, cravings, and help and service, writes the journal entry, and finds a recovery meeting anywhere, any time. The person never fills in a form. An SOS screen lists their support contacts in order, with one tap to call or text and a way to log whether they reached someone. The AI never has the last word. It shows the text it heard and everything it marked off, and the person can edit or override any of it.

Result

A side consulting project for a recovery coach and their clients, handed over to the coach. The working version runs the full daily plan from voice, with a weekly summary and trends over time. The idea is that each person leaves treatment with their own app, set up from their plan in about 15 minutes.

▸Personal Agent Operating SystemAn inbox that arrives already triagedScheduled agent
Chief of staff · Built with Claude

Problem

Email and a to-do list grow faster than one person can clear them, and most of the work inside them is sorting, digging and drafting.

How AI is used

Every morning an agent reads the whole inbox, archives what does not need me and sends back takeaways on each newsletter. For everything else it opens the attachment, checks my calendar and reads the source for the real deadline, then recommends a call with a confidence level. I answer in one word: Draft, Research, Mine, Kill or Park. Decisions land as Gmail drafts, Calendar holds and Todoist tasks, sorted by area. On Fridays it reshapes next week's Todoist queue from my job search pipeline. Nothing is sent or created without my yes.

Result

I start with the sorting and digging done and spend my time on decisions. The instructions live in a Drive doc and a git repo, not in app settings, so every session runs the same playbook. Next step, not built yet: tag a Todoist task @agent and the agent preps it and hands it back as @review.

Same pattern at work: a shared team inbox, a support queue or an approvals queue.

▸Fantasy FootballA week of decisions answered in one lineScheduled agent
Fantasy-Football · Built with Claude

Problem

Two leagues on two sites, with different rules and opposite goals: a keeper league building for next year and a family league playing to win now. Keeping up with injuries, waivers and trade deadlines in both is a weekly chore.

How AI is used

Claude runs on a schedule, three times a week, with no prompt from me. Each league is a markdown file of rules, scoring, keeper math and that league's goal, and every run reads it first. Claude reads both sites, the injury news and the waiver pool, makes the safe moves itself and turns everything else into a short numbered list with a confidence level on each item. It never adds, drops, trades or messages anyone without my yes.

Result

Each week becomes one list I answer in a line, like “yes 3, 5, no 4.” Deadlines show up on their date as a single decision. Every run and every answer is written to a new log file, so the git history becomes the season.

Same pattern at work: any recurring review where most moves are routine and a few need a person.

Problem

Two people shopping and planning from one list, on phones where the work network blocks most apps.

How AI is used

Banjo Uke Songbook · Built with Claude

Problem

I wanted to practice banjo uke and fingerpicking songs from my phone on a music stand, without jumping between videos, tab sites and lyrics sites.
